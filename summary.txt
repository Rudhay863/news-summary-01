World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026:

1. US-mediated talks between Russia and Ukraine ended in Abu Dhabi with agreement on a large prisoner-of-war exchange but no ceasefire or deal on territory (Reuters). US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff next meet Vladimir Putin in Moscow.
2. The COP31 leaders' summit opened in Antalya, Turkey, where UN chief Antonio Guterres warned the slow climate response had put the world "on a path to destruction" (AFP). The US sent no officials.
3. Israeli airstrikes killed three people in Gaza, including two brothers in a tent in Sheikh Radwan; Gaza health officials say over 1,600 Palestinians have died since the ceasefire (Reuters).
4. The US Justice Department charged three alleged Iranian operatives over a campaign of nearly 20 attacks in Europe and the US, including a September plot against RAF Fairford in England; Iran denies involvement (BBC).
5. The US House voted 211-204 to send eight articles impeaching Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, blocking an immediate vote; only Republican Thomas Massie opposed it (Reuters).
6. Democrat Scott Colom defeated Republican Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi's Senate race, 49.19% to 48.71%, becoming the state's first Black senator since Reconstruction; Democrats now hold 55 Senate seats (AP).
7. US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October and core prices 0.3%, both above forecasts, leaving a December Fed rate rise fully priced; no annual rate was published (BLS).
8. Democratic Republic of Congo's Ebola outbreak passed 11,042 cases and 5,387 deaths, its deadliest ever; the Bundibugyo virus has no approved vaccine (AP).
9. UNICEF warned that drones and drought were "closing in" on Sudan's children: up to 15 power stations were hit in October and drones caused two in three child casualties in 2026 (UNICEF).
10. Bulgaria held a national day of mourning for 12 people killed in fog pile-ups involving 86 vehicles on the Trakia motorway (BBC).
